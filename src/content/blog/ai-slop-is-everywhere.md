Given my background in machine learning and content creation, I've become quite adept at recognizing AI patterns.

AI slop is everywhere. By everywhere, I truly mean *everywhere*.

To name a couple examples: YouTube videos, billboards, social media posts, commercials, T-shirts, posters, websites... These are just a few I have seen in the past 24 hours.

I'm convinced no matter who you are, interacting with AI-generated content is unavoidable. Do this enough, and you run into two consequences.

First, you lose trust in the content you consume.

When I recognize a content creator is presenting AI-generated text as their own words, I begin to doubt all of their other opinions and conclusions. Surprisingly, I don't see sarcastic comments on these pieces "thanking Claude" anymore. I see comments like:

- "I wish I had this three months ago when I was going through *x*."
- "This is so insightful."
- "Wise words."

Aside: Whether those comments are written by a human is a separate conversation.

We are inundated with AI at every turn, and most of us aren't conscious of it. I doubt any of us completes a day without seeing thousands of individually AI-generated items.

This leads me to the second consequence: I don't believe all of this AI slop is actually generated by AI.

We are heavily influenced by our environments. Our outputs are in large part a product of our inputs.

Whether or not we turn to an AI chatbot for assistance, with so much slop surrounding us, it would be impossible for it to not seep into our own creations. Even if we could perfectly identify AI content, I still doubt we would be able to consciously reject it from influencing us.

This creates a positive reinforcement loop. The more AI slop we consume, the more AI slop we produce, increasing the amount of AI slop there is to consume.

Perhaps it will take additional AI advancement to break the cycle. Perhaps something else.

But, in the meantime, I will not permit an AI to construct my words. You can be certain that this and future articles will be my own voice and ideas.

And if an AI pattern in my writing gives you reason to doubt that, it's probably because AI slop is everywhere.
